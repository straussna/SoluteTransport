Papers

The reports and articles behind the solute transport model that SoluteTransport implements, grouped by subject. Each entry links its PDF in docs/reference/papers/, or its DOI where no free copy exists.

OTIS model

seven

OTIS (One-Dimensional Transport with Inflow and Storage) is the model whose equations the library solves: advection, dispersion, lateral inflow, transient storage and sorption.

YearReferenceContents
1991Runkel, R.L., and Broshears, R.E. One-Dimensional Transport with Inflow and Storage (OTIS): A Solute Transport Model for Small Streams. CADSWES, University of Colorado, final report in cooperation with the USGS.The original model formulation and numerical solution.
1991Runkel, R.L., and Broshears, R.E. Appendices A–D of the 1991 report, web edition on the USGS OTIS site, updated to match the current OTIS documentation.Derivation of the governing equations, finite-difference approximations and boundary conditions, Crank–Nicolson matrix coefficients, and steady-state matrix coefficients.
1998Runkel, R.L. One-Dimensional Transport with Inflow and Storage (OTIS): A Solute Transport Model for Streams and Rivers. U.S. Geological Survey Water-Resources Investigations Report 98-4018.The user manual: governing equations, input files, OTIS-P parameter estimation and worked applications.
1999Runkel, R.L., Bencala, K.E., and Kimball, B.A. Modeling solute transport and geochemistry in streams and rivers using OTIS and OTEQ. In U.S. Geological Survey Toxic Substances Hydrology Program, Proceedings of the Technical Meeting, Water-Resources Investigations Report 99-4018A, 109–118.OTIS and OTEQ applications to tracer transport, trace-metal removal and pH-modification experiments. The PDF holds only these pages of the proceedings volume.
2000Runkel, R.L. Using OTIS to model solute transport in streams and rivers. U.S. Geological Survey Fact Sheet FS-138-99.A four-page overview of the processes OTIS simulates and how it is applied.
2010Runkel, R.L. One-Dimensional Transport with Equilibrium Chemistry (OTEQ): A reactive transport model for streams and rivers. U.S. Geological Survey Techniques and Methods, Book 6, Chapter B6.The OTEQ manual: OTIS transport coupled with MINTEQ equilibrium chemistry, including tests of the downstream boundary condition.
2009Runkel, R.L. Quantifying Transient Storage and Hyporheic Exchange using Tracer Techniques and the OTIS Solute Transport Model. NABS Workshop, Grand Rapids, Michigan, 17 May 2009.Workshop notes on tracer experiments and fitting OTIS to them.

Transport theory and analytical solutions

four

These derive the advection-dispersion and transient-storage equations and give closed-form solutions for checking numerical results.

YearReferenceContents
1993Runkel, R.L., and Chapra, S.C. An efficient numerical solution of the transient storage equations for solute transport in small streams. Water Resources Research 29(1), 211–215. doi:10.1029/92WR02217 (no free copy; publisher access only).The decoupled implicit scheme OTIS uses to solve the main-channel and storage-zone equations.
1995Runkel, R.L., and Bencala, K.E. Transport of reacting solutes in rivers and streams. In V.P. Singh (ed.), Environmental Hydrology, Kluwer Academic Publishers, 137–164.The processes acting on solutes in streams and the equations that describe them.
1996Runkel, R.L. Solution of the Advection-Dispersion Equation: Continuous Load of Finite Duration. Journal of Environmental Engineering 122(9), 830–832.The exact analytical solution for an upstream release of finite duration.
2005De Smedt, F., Brevis, W., and Debels, P. Analytical solution for solute transport resulting from instantaneous injection in streams with transient storage. Journal of Hydrology 315, 25–39. doi:10.1016/j.jhydrol.2005.04.002An analytical transient-storage solution for a slug injection, verified against OTIS.

Transient storage and nutrient uptake

two

These interpret fitted transient-storage parameters and use them to separate transport from nutrient uptake.

YearReferenceContents
2002Runkel, R.L. A new metric for determining the importance of transient storage. Journal of the North American Benthological Society 21(4), 529–543.Fmed, the fraction of median reach travel time due to transient storage.
2007Runkel, R.L. Toward a transport-based analysis of nutrient spiraling and uptake in streams. Limnology and Oceanography: Methods 5, 50–62.Nutrient spiraling metrics computed from transport model parameters.

National Institute of Standards and Technology Internal Report NBSIR 86-3448, text revised 15 March 1990. Excerpt hosted on the USGS OTIS site.The portions of the STARPAC guide that apply to OTIS-P.
